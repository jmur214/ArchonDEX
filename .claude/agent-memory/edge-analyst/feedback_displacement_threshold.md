When an idea adds an asset or strategy X by **displacing** equity, first-order log growth changes by
dg/dw ≈ μ_X − μ_E + σ_E² − ρσ_Eσ_X at small weight w.
- With planning μ_E ≈ 10 % and σ_E ≈ 16 %, an uncorrelated X must earn > **7.4 %/yr**.
- A crash-correlated X must earn more. Put-writing (ρ ≈ 0.85, σ ≈ 10 %) needs ≈ 8.8 %.

A **stacked** overlay (no displacement, cheap or no financing) needs μ_O > ρσ_Eσ_O + kσ_O² instead.

**Why:**
- This one line explains most of the record's "drawdown better, wealth worse" results: gold (B-10), the trend sleeve (T-311/T-333), the barbell (T-251 −1.6 %/yr), CEF, and merger-arb-style bills-plus-premium strategies.
- It was derived for T-410 (2026-11-20) when the owner asked how to "combine strategies into a better machine". The answer for this owner (max terminal wealth, never sells) is to stack, not to blend or displace.

**How to apply:**
- Put this inequality in the first paragraph of any diversifier, income-leg or "add a sleeve" brief, before computing power.
- If X fails it, the only live form is stacked with financing cheaper than X's excess. That is the leverage belief decision (T-409), not an edge test.
- It is planning arithmetic only: state μ_E, and never quote it as a measurement.
