# An integrated metrics stack for retail algorithmic traders

**Bottom line up front.** There is no single most-informative metric, and there never was. **Raw Sharpe is the default headline only because traders kept reaching for the familiar tool while the literature moved on.** What changes outcomes for a retail algorithmic trader is a layered stack of metrics in which each layer answers a different question: (1) is there an edge that survives multiple-testing deflation; (2) is the portfolio's tail and diversification structure honest; (3) does the backtest pass selection-bias and leakage gates; (4) is live execution eating the alpha. Across the four layers, the metrics that actually move decisions are the **Deflated Sharpe Ratio, Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio with the Lo autocorrelation correction, expectancy net of costs, PBO via Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation, Minimum Backtest Length, purged-and-embargoed cross-validation, the realized-vs-target volatility ratio, Expected Shortfall at 97.5%, the effective number of bets, and an Implementation Shortfall decomposition with explicit price-improvement-vs-NBBO accounting**. The metrics that retail content celebrates and that this report rejects on technical grounds are **raw Sharpe without adjustments, win rate as a quality signal, profit factor on small samples, naive 95% VaR, max drawdown as a point estimate, in-sample R²/CAGR, and "robustness" claims based on parameter perturbation**. The rest of this report justifies every one of those calls from primary literature and prescribes thresholds with derivation methodology.

# PART 1 — Framework

## 1.1 Metrics are decision support, not scoring

A retail algorithmic trader makes a small number of consequential decisions: **deploy a new edge, retire an active edge, size up, size down, rebalance across sleeves, investigate a degraded live behavior, accept or reject a backtest.** A metric earns its place in the dashboard only if it directly changes one of those actions. Anything else is reporting theatre — and reporting theatre is not free, because it dilutes attention and inflates the count of significance tests being run silently in the trader's head, which directly worsens the multiple-testing problem documented by Harvey, Liu and Zhu (RFS 2016) and López de Prado's PBO/DSR work.

The decisions are not symmetric. **Deploy-or-not** is the most consequential and the most exposed to backtest-overfitting; the relevant evidence base is the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, PBO, MBL, and purged cross-validation. **Retire-or-not** is dominated by alpha-decay detection — CUSUM, structural break tests, and a pre-registered kill threshold. **Sizing** is dominated by realized-vs-target volatility, Expected Shortfall, drawdown vs Magdon-Ismail expected MDD, and Kelly bounds. **Rebalancing** is dominated by sleeve-level correlation, the effective number of bets, and conditional tail-correlation in stressed windows. **Escalating a live investigation** is dominated by the paper-vs-live implementation-shortfall gap decomposed into delay, execution, and opportunity components. Each layer of metrics below exists to inform exactly one of these decisions; metrics that don't are excluded.

## 1.2 Principles of good metric design

A defensible metric is **predictive rather than purely descriptive** (it forecasts future performance, not just summarizes the past); **robust rather than gameable**; **multiple-testing-aware** (it knows you tried many variants); **a confidence interval rather than a point estimate**; **out-of-sample rather than in-sample**; and **honest about the distinction between statistical and economic significance**. Almost every popular metric fails at least one of these tests. Raw Sharpe is descriptive, not predictive once one knows the strategy was selected from a parameter grid. Win rate is gameable in two clicks (tight target, wide stop). Max drawdown is a single-realization point estimate of a quantity whose theoretical mean, per Magdon-Ismail & Atiya (J. Applied Probability 2004), grows as √T under zero drift — meaning your worst-ever drawdown is statistically *likely* to be exceeded if you keep trading.

The cleanest single principle: **a metric should be reported as a confidence interval together with the trial count that produced it, and judged against a deflated threshold rather than an absolute one.** This is the López de Prado / Bailey reform, and it has not yet penetrated retail content.

## 1.3 The four-layer stack and how layers interact

The four layers are not independent. **Slippage at the execution layer (layer 4) erodes backtest-implied Sharpe at the edge layer (layer 1), which compresses both the Probabilistic Sharpe and the Deflated Sharpe at the backtest-validity layer (layer 3), which shows up at the portfolio layer (layer 2) as a smaller realized risk-adjusted return than the model predicted.** The diagnostic protocol therefore runs in the opposite direction of the typical retail workflow: when live underperforms paper, you first ask whether the execution layer ate the alpha (Implementation Shortfall decomposition), then whether the edge has decayed (CUSUM and rolling PSR), then whether the original backtest was overfit (recompute DSR with the now-known trial count), and only last whether the portfolio construction is wrong. Retail traders typically debug in the reverse, sub-optimal order.

## 1.4 The distributional math that retail content skips

Return distributions are not Gaussian. Equity strategies in particular have **autocorrelated returns** (positive at short horizons from microstructure and at longer horizons from smoothing/illiquid marks; negative at multi-day horizons from mean-reversion); **negative skew** in directional and option-writing strategies; **excess kurtosis** in every realistic asset class; and **regime dependence** in moments that are estimated on calm-period data and then used to size positions for crisis periods.

Each property breaks the standard metrics in a specific way. **Positive autocorrelation inflates the naive annualized Sharpe** by reducing the measured period-volatility relative to the true volatility of aggregate returns; Lo (FAJ 2002) shows the inflation factor is η(q) = q / √[q + 2·Σ(q − k)·ρ_k] vs the naive √q, and empirically demonstrates hedge fund Sharpes overstated by ~65% when ρ₁ ≈ 0.34 is ignored. **Negative skew with positive premium** (selling puts, carry, mean-reversion) produces high in-sample Sharpe that systematically misrepresents tail risk; Goetzmann, Ingersoll, Spiegel & Welch (RFS 2007) prove that any non-MPPM performance measure including Sharpe, Sortino, and Jensen's alpha can be manipulated upward by selling out-of-the-money options. **Fat tails** invalidate the parametric standard errors of every moment-based statistic; sample kurtosis is itself not consistent under power-law tails of index α<8 (Cohen, Davis, Samorodnitsky 2020). **Regime dependence** means correlation matrices estimated in calm regimes mechanically understate tail risk; Longin & Solnik (J. Finance 2001) document that equity-equity correlations rise sharply in negative-tail events but not in positive-tail events.

The corollary is that **a typical equity strategy's headline annualized Sharpe is inflated by its own structure**, and a short-vol or premium-harvesting strategy's headline Sharpe is *guaranteed* to look good in samples that exclude the tail event the strategy is implicitly short. Any adjustment that ignores skewness/kurtosis (raw Sharpe, raw Sortino, raw IR) is technically insufficient.

**Where canonical recommendations have been challenged out-of-sample.** Raw Sharpe was canonical for thirty years and is now formally dominated by PSR/DSR. Naive 95% VaR was canonical and is now replaced by 97.5% Expected Shortfall in Basel III FRTB. The Almgren–Chriss square-root impact law was canonical and is empirically a 3/5 power law in the Almgren et al. 2005 calibration, with Toth et al. 2011 / Kyle–Obizhaeva 2016 reaffirming the square-root form under a different invariance argument — there is no settled answer, and the bid-ask spread dominates at retail scale anyway. PBO itself has been criticized for sensitivity to the partition count S and for being gameable by adding dilutive trials, per the original Bailey/Borwein/LdP/Zhu paper.

**Synthesis — the eight edge metrics that survive the cut.** PSR with the Lo η(q) correction baked in. DSR with honest N. Expectancy in R, net of costs, with bootstrap CI. Calmar paired with Ulcer Index for sizing only. Pre-registered post-deployment decay monitor (CUSUM on standardized returns + rolling PSR). Capacity check via square-root impact law for traders trading >$few-hundred-K or in microcaps/options. Everything else is diagnostic. **Win rate is excluded as a quality metric**; it is reported only as a behavioral diagnostic.

| **Realized vs target volatility** | R = σ_realized / σ_target, EWMA λ=0.94 or rolling 20/60-day. | **Most actionable lever**: R > 1.2 → cut by 1/R; R < 0.8 → relever (capped). | EWMA stabilizes in ~60 days; both 20 and 60 day windows. | Slow vs jumps; vol-of-vol can chase tail in trending regimes. | GARCH(1,1) or EWMA forecast; cap leverage scale-up. | Realized variance from HF data; implied (VIX/MOVE) overlay. | **Include — single most actionable.** |
| **Sleeve correlation matrix** | ρ_ij = Cov/(σ_iσ_j). | Rebalance; derisk when correlations spike. | ~250 obs for a single ρ near 0.4; **T ≥ 10·N² for a stable N×N matrix** to avoid Marchenko-Pastur noise. | Pearson is linear; sample matrix below 10N² is ill-conditioned (inverse hyper-sensitive); correlations rise in negative tail per Longin-Solnik (2001). | Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage; random-matrix filtering; Spearman/Kendall for nonlinear. | Tail dependence (next); DCC-GARCH. | **Include** with shrinkage; always pair with stressed-window conditional. |
| **Effective number of bets** | **Meucci N_Ent** = exp(−Σ p_i ln p_i) on principal-portfolio variance contributions (Meucci 2009); HHI(w) = Σw_i², N_w = 1/HHI; HHI(RC) on risk contributions; Diversification Ratio = (wᵀσ)/√(wᵀΣw) (Choueifaty-Coignard 2008); Carver IDM = √N capped at 2.5. | Diversification monitoring; rebalance trigger when N_Ent drops 50% from median. | T ≥ 10·N² with shrinkage. | Σ instability in crisis (PCs collapse → N_Ent drops mechanically, which is actually the correct alert). | Shrinkage; PCA. | — | **Include — N_Ent is the right portfolio diversification statistic.** |
| **Leverage / gross & net exposure** | Gross = Σ|pos|/NAV; net = (Σlongs−Σshorts)/NAV; Reg-T 2:1 overnight, FINRA Rule 4210 maintenance 25%, **PDT $25K floor eliminated by SEC SR-FINRA-2025-017 approval April 14 2026, effective June 4 2026** (intraday margin framework replaces it); portfolio margin ~6.7:1 on diversified books ≥$100K. | Hard caps; daily. | Daily. | Overnight gap → forced liquidation; margin requirements jump in stress. | Stress test with −15% long / +15% short assumption. | Notional (delta-adjusted) for options/futures. | **Include — daily, headline.** |
| **Beta to market** | β = Cov(R_p, R_m)/Var(R_m); rolling 60-day; β_up and β_down separately (Ang-Chen-Xing 2006 down-risk asymmetry). | Market-neutrality monitoring. | 60 obs noisy (SE 0.15–0.25); 250 for SE<0.1. | Single-factor; unstable in stress. | Fama-French-Carhart multi-factor; DCC-GARCH conditional β. | — | **Include but split** β_up / β_down. |
| **Skewness** | Pearson γ₁ = E[(R−μ)³]/σ³; Bowley = (Q3+Q1−2Q2)/(Q3−Q1) robust. | Discount SR if γ₁ < −1: option-writing, carry, mean-reversion silently short tails. | SE(γ₁) ≈ √(6/n); n=250 → SE≈0.15 under normality; diverges under fat tails. | Single outlier moves it dramatically. | Bootstrap CI; report both Pearson and Bowley. | Cornish-Fisher VaR/ES adjust. | **Include** — track always. **Rule (estimated): γ₁ < −1 with SR > 1 → discount SR by 50% before sizing.** |
| **Excess kurtosis** | γ₂ = E[(R−μ)⁴]/σ⁴ − 3. | Qualitative only; trigger heavy-tail modeling. | SE ≈ √(24/n); not consistent under heavy tails (Cohen-Davis-Samorodnitsky 2020). | Dominated by single obs; diverges under heavy tails. | Tail index / Hill estimator; Student-t fit (ν df). | ES; max single-period loss. | **Exclude as a point-estimate decision input**; use Student-t or ES. |
| **Max single-period loss** | L_max = max(−R_t). Under normality, expected worst day in T trials ≈ σ·Φ⁻¹(1−1/T); T=252 → 2.74σ; T=2500 → 3.49σ. | Sanity check on all parametric risk numbers. | None. | Path-dependent on rebalancing; stale marks. | Pair with ES; intraday max-DD if relevant. | Block-maxima EVT. | **Include — impossible to game.** **Rule: realized worst day > 5σ ⇒ vol model is broken.** |
| **Tail dependence / conditional correlation** | λ_L = lim_{u→0+} C(u,u)/u for copula C; Gaussian copula has λ=0 for any ρ<1 (Salmon 2009 "the formula that killed Wall Street"); t-copula has λ > 0. Retail proxy: Pearson ρ on worst 5% of market days. | Diversification reality check; tail hedge sizing. | ≥ 500 obs for empirical λ; parametric t-copula more efficient. | Threshold sensitivity; few tail obs. | Fit t-copula or Clayton; bootstrap CI. | DCC-GARCH; exceedance correlation curves (Ang-Chen 2002). | **Include** — conditional ρ in worst-decile days is the retail-feasible version and more honest than the unconditional matrix. |

**Synthesis — the 7-number portfolio dashboard.** Weekly: (1) **realized/target vol ratio R**, (2) **gross & net leverage**, (3) **Meucci N_Ent**, (4) **underwater curve + current DD vs Magdon-Ismail E[MDD]**. Monthly: (5) **ES_0.975 daily historical**, (6) **conditional ρ in worst-decile market days**, (7) **per-sleeve {Sharpe, skewness, max single-period loss}**. Optional #8 for traders running allocation optimization: **CDaR_0.95 as the drawdown constraint**, never raw MDD because MDD is path-dependent and not convex in weights. **VaR, raw kurtosis as a point estimate, raw Calmar, and Sortino-in-addition-to-Sharpe are excluded as decoration.**

## 2.3 Layer 3 — Backtest validity

| Method | Definition | Decision use | Sample / data | Failure modes | Threshold | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Minimum Backtest Length (MBL)** | MBL_years ≈ 2·ln(N) / E[max SR_annual]² (Bailey-Borwein-LdP-Zhu, Notices AMS 2014). N=45 trials at target SR=1 → 7.6 yr; N=100 → 9.2 yr; N=1000 → 13.8 yr. | **Front-door check.** If your dataset is shorter than MBL given honest N, you are guaranteed to overfit. | Trivially computable; requires honest N. | Assumes independent Gaussian trials (use PCA for correlated). Necessary, not sufficient. | T_years ≥ MBL(N, target SR) | **Include — non-negotiable first gate.** |
| **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)** | See edge layer. Bailey-LdP JPM 2014. | Deploy / reject gate after selection. | T ≥ 500 daily; honest trial log; V[{SR̂_n}] from ≥ 30 trials. | Sensitive to assumed N (analyst honesty); parametric; Gaussian-corrected by 3rd/4th moments only. | DSR ≥ 0.95. *Retail implication: 5-yr daily backtest with ~50 effective trials needs annualized SR ≈ 1.2 just to clear DSR null; most retail "Sharpe 1" backtests fail.* | **Include — non-negotiable.** |
| **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)** | CSCV (Bailey-Borwein-LdP-Zhu JoCF 2017): partition T×N matrix into S submatrices, take all C(S, S/2) IS/OOS combinations, compute fraction of splits where IS-optimal underperforms OOS median. | Model-free overfitting diagnostic. | Full T×N matrix for ALL trials; T/S ≥ 50; S=16 typical. | Sensitive to S; gameable by adding dilutive trials; doesn't catch look-ahead / regime / wrong universe. | **PBO < 0.5** (deploy); **< 0.3** preferred; > 0.8 ⇒ reject; report S used. | **Include — non-negotiable.** |
| **Walk-forward efficiency (WFE)** | OOS performance / IS performance, rolled across ≥ 6 windows (Pardo 2008). | Sanity check after optimization. | ≥ 30 trades per OOS window. | Single-window luck; gameable via window-length tuning. | WFE ≥ 0.5–0.6; ≥ 70% windows profitable. | **Include with caveats** — sanity check, not test. |
| **Harvey-Liu haircut** | Apply Bonferroni/Holm/BHY to factor t-stat; back out adjusted SR (Harvey-Liu JPM 2015). | Sizing the "publication discount" before deploying. | Need N and ideally prior p-value distribution. | "Industry 50% haircut" is wrong; haircut > 80% for SR < 0.4, ≤ 25% for SR > 1.0. | Adjusted t > 3 (HLZ RFS 2016). | **Include.** |
| **Purged k-fold + embargoed CV** | López de Prado AFML Ch. 7: purge training observations whose event time [t0, t1] overlaps test set; embargo h % training obs immediately after test. | Mandatory for ML strategies and any overlapping-label strategy. | Need labels with explicit event times t1[i]. | Embargo size heuristic; regime leakage beyond embargo; expensive (CPCV especially). | All folds positive; embargo ≥ max label horizon. | **Include — non-negotiable for ML / overlapping labels.** |
| **CPCV (Combinatorial Purged CV)** | López de Prado AFML Ch. 12: N groups, test on k at a time, C(N,k) splits → Φ = k·C(N,k)/N OOS paths → distribution of OOS Sharpes. | Feed OOS Sharpes into DSR/PBO. | Computationally expensive. | Same as purged k-fold. | DSR ≥ 0.95 on OOS path distribution. | **Include** for serious validation. |
| **Multiple-testing correction** | Bonferroni FWER: reject p_i ≤ α/M. Holm sequential. BH FDR: reject p_(i) ≤ (i/M)α. BHY for dependent tests (Harvey-Liu-Zhu RFS 2016 supports BHY for finance). | Factor / strategy screening. FDR for screening; FWER for capital allocation. | M = number of tests. | Bonferroni over-conservative when M large. | **t > 3.0** per HLZ-RFS 2016; Bonferroni alone implied 3.78 by 2012, projected 4.0 by 2032. | **Include — non-negotiable.** |
| **Stationary block bootstrap (Politis-Romano 1994)** | Geometric block length L with E[L]=1/p; resample to length T; recompute statistic; B=1,000–10,000 replications. Politis-White data-driven L. | CI on Sharpe, MDD, expectancy. | T ≥ 500. | Block-length sensitivity; SB preserves stationarity, breaks regimes. | 95% CI on SR excludes 0. | **Include.** |
| **Survivorship bias detection** | Point-in-time databases (CRSP, Compustat PIT, Norgate); audit 10 random delisted names. Empirical bias ~1.6% annually for equities; 4–6% for mutual funds. | Binary: pass or invalidate. | — | If universe grows monotonically through history → bias present. | No threshold; binary. | **Include — non-negotiable.** |
| **Look-ahead detection** | FRED vs ALFRED vintages for macro; PIT fundamentals; verify timestamps. | Binary. | — | Silent leaks: GDP/CPI revisions, analyst-estimate revisions, index reconstitution announcement vs effective dates. | Binary. | **Include — non-negotiable.** |
| **Reality Check / SPA / Romano-Wolf** | White (2000) Econometrica: T_RC = max √n·d̄_k; bootstrap null. Hansen (2005) SPA: studentized, recenters competitive models only. Romano-Wolf (2005) stepwise: iterates max statistic with bootstrap quantiles. | Test best-of-many strategies against null. | Full universe of M strategies' returns. | RC conservative w/ many irrelevant models; SPA studentization sensitivity. | adjusted p < 0.05. | **Include** for any large strategy search. |
| **Parameter plateau** | Heat-map; plateau vs cliff. | Visual robustness. | — | "Robustness via perturbation" is a gameable proxy — neighbors on the same data are correlated and don't test OOS. | Plateau ≥ 20% of grid range with all metrics ≥ 70% of peak. | **Include with caveats** — not a substitute for DSR/PBO. |
| **Monte Carlo significance / permutation** | Permute signal-return pairing; bootstrap returns; recompute statistic. | Test null of "signal = noise." | T ≥ 500. | Permutation too aggressive under autocorrelated signal. | p < 0.05. | **Include.** |

**Synthesis — the 12-gate validity protocol.** No strategy enters paper trading without passing every gate, in order: (0) PIT data confirmed → (1) T_years ≥ 2 ln(N) / SR_target² → (2) trial count N declared and logged → (3) adjusted t > 3.0 → (4) DSR ≥ 0.95 → (5) PBO < 0.5 with S=16 reported → (6) purged k-fold + embargo, all folds positive → (7) WFE ≥ 0.5 with ≥ 70% windows profitable → (8) RC/SPA p < 0.05 → (9) bootstrap 95% CI on SR excludes 0 → (10) parameter plateau ≥ 20% grid → (11) post-Bonferroni/BHY haircut SR clears target → (12) economic mechanism articulable a priori. **The practical implication for retail: a typical 5-parameter × 5-value grid on 5 years of daily data fails MBL outright (needs ~9 years), and most "Sharpe 1" retail backtests fail DSR. Multi-asset / multi-decade datasets are the only realistic path through.**

## 2.4 Layer 4 — Execution / TCA (Alpaca retail)

| Metric | Definition | Decision use | Retail computability | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Implementation Shortfall (Perold 1988)** | IS = (paper return) − (real return), decomposed: explicit fees + delay cost (P_arrival − P_decision)·X + execution cost (P_fill − P_arrival)·x_filled + opportunity cost (P_terminal − P_decision)·(X−x_filled). | All-in measure; top of the TCA funnel. | High — you control every timestamp. **Use bar-close that triggered the signal as P_decision.** | **Include — top-tier.** |
| **Slippage vs decision price** | (P_fill − P_decision)·side, bps of P_decision. | Detects signal staleness. | High. | **Include.** |
| **Slippage vs arrival price** | (VWAP_fill − P_arrival)·side, P_arrival = mid at submit. Kissell-Glantz benchmark. | Isolates execution leg from delay. | Medium — without sub-second NBBO, 1-min bar mid injects O(½ spread) noise. | **Include**; treat as noisy. |
| **Slippage vs VWAP/TWAP** | (VWAP_fill − VWAP_market)·side over execution window. | Only useful when slicing. | Medium-high via 1-min bar VW field. | **Skip** for unsliced single-clip retail (gameable by trading passively with terrible IS). |
| **Fill rate** | Filled / submitted by order type. | Headline for limit-order strategies; market orders should ~100%. | Trivial via Alpaca order events. | **Include.** |
| **Partial-fill analysis** | Distribution of (filled/submitted) by ADV bucket, spread, time-of-day. | Liquidity bottleneck detection. | High. | **Include at low priority** — rarely binding at retail scale. |
| **Market impact (Almgren-Chriss)** | Linear: permanent γv, temporary ηv. Almgren et al. 2005: temporary impact ∝ σ·(v/V)^0.6 (rejects pure square-root). Toth 2011 / Kyle-Obizhaeva 2016 reaffirm √Q under invariance. | Pre-trade cost; scheduling. | **Low value at retail.** Retail Q/V is 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵; modeled impact 0.01–0.1 bps, 2–3 orders of magnitude below half-spread. | **Skip** at retail; revisit only for microcaps where clip > 1% bar volume. |
| **Time-to-execution (latency)** | (t_fill − t_signal), with sub-decomposition. | Bounds horizon of viable strategies. | High with disciplined client logging. **Alpaca typical end-to-end 100ms–1s — strategies with signal half-life < 10s are on the wrong infra.** | **Include.** |
| **Effective / realized spread (Bessembinder 2003)** | Effective = 2·|P_trade − M_t|/M_t; realized = 2·D·(P_trade − M_{t+Δ})/M_t at Δ=5 min; price impact = effective − realized. | The dominant cost at retail; quantifies wholesaler price improvement. | Medium — without paid SIP, use 1-min bar (H+L)/2 as midpoint proxy with O(2–5 bps) noise. | **Include — top-tier.** |
| **Realized commissions/fees vs modeled** | Alpaca (Apr 2026 schedule): $0 commission; SEC §31 0.206 bps sells only; FINRA TAF $0.000195/share sells only (cap $9.79); CAT currently $0; options ORF $0.02295, OCC $0.025, TAF $0.00329 per contract. Hidden cost: PFOF flows to broker (2Q2019 606 disclosed ~$0.0020/share from Wolverine; venues Virtu/Citadel/Jane Street/Two Sigma/Wolverine). Battalio-Corwin-Jennings 2016 J. Finance: broker rebate chase ⇒ worse limit-order execution. | Sanity check fee model; PFOF/price-improvement is the real cost. | Trivial. | **Include** as sanity check. Modeling spread > modeling fees by far. |
| **Opportunity cost of unfilled orders** | (X_unfilled/X)·(P_terminal − P_decision)·side. | Catches "winners that didn't fill" — biggest silent killer of limit strategies. | High. | **Include — essential for limit-order strategies.** |
| **Paper-vs-live diagnostic protocol** | Replay live trade list inside backtester with forced fills → residual is backtest unrealism + signal staleness; aggregate IS slippage decomposed into delay/execution; fill rate by paper-return bucket (high-return bucket with low fill rate ⇒ opportunity cost dominates); re-run backtest with realistic frictions (½ × spread per leg, volume rejection, 1-bar submit delay, stochastic limit fills). | **The retail TCA workflow.** | High. | **Include — central.** |

**Synthesis — the 6-metric retail TCA dashboard.** (1) IS with full 4-way decomposition; (2) effective half-spread realized vs quoted at submit; (3) slippage decomposed into delay (P_arrival − P_decision) and execution (P_fill − P_arrival); (4) fill rate by order type plus opportunity cost on unfilled; (5) time-to-execution distribution; (6) the paper-vs-live diagnostic protocol. **Skip:** Almgren-Chriss / market impact modeling at retail (Q/V too small); VWAP/TWAP for single-clip orders; detailed partial-fill analytics. **Operational reality:** Alpaca's free IEX-only feed is not NBBO and routinely misses the inside price by a tick or more; for meaningful effective-spread TCA, subscribe to the SIP feed. The bid-ask half-spread is the dominant retail cost — at large-cap names 1–5 bps after decimalization (Bessembinder 2003 JFQA), 10–50+ bps at small-cap names.

# PART 3 — Do-not-use list and decision matrix

## 3.1 Do-not-use (or use only with explicit caveats)

| Metric | Failure mode | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw Sharpe without Lo / DSR / PSR adjustment** | Inflated by positive autocorrelation up to 65% (Lo FAJ 2002); gameable by selling tails (Goetzmann-Ingersoll-Spiegel-Welch RFS 2007 prove every non-MPPM measure is manipulable); inflated by selection bias from N silent trials (Bailey-LdP JPM 2014). | **PSR + DSR** with Lo η(q) on top; deploy threshold DSR ≥ 0.95. |
| **Win rate as a quality measure** | Trivially gameable via tight-target/wide-stop; high win rate with negative expectancy is the Martingale forex pattern; binomial CI half-width at N=100 is ±9.8% — meaningless. | **Expectancy in R units, net of costs**, with bootstrap CI. |
| **Profit factor on small samples** | Ratio of heavy-tailed sums; one trade can dominate; removing top losses can move PF from 1.1 to 2.0; gross-of-costs. Need ≥ 100 trades, ideally ≥ 200. | **Bootstrap CI on PF**, "PF excluding top 5%" stress, or just trade-level Sharpe. |
| **Max drawdown without volatility context** | Single realization of a sup-statistic with E[MDD] ∝ √T, log T, or T depending on drift (Magdon-Ismail-Atiya 2004); biased downward. Cherry-pickable via window choice and monthly-vs-daily marks. | Compare to **Magdon-Ismail E[MDD] given measured σ and SR**; pair with Ulcer Index and CDaR. |
| **In-sample R², in-sample CAGR, "robustness" via parameter perturbation** | Pseudo-mathematics (Bailey-Borwein-LdP-Zhu Notices AMS 2014); parameter neighbors are correlated, not OOS. | **CSCV / PBO**, purged-embargoed CV, walk-forward with WFE ≥ 0.5 and ≥ 70% positive windows. |
| **Single-backtest "out-of-sample" without proper holdout protocol** | Once the hold-out has been peeked at, it's IS. Harvey-Liu JPM 2015 critique. | **Purged k-fold + embargo CV; CPCV with DSR on OOS path Sharpes.** |
| **Returns without realistic transaction costs and slippage** | Novy-Marx-Velikov RFS 2016: most published anomalies with >50% monthly turnover die after realistic costs. | Net of bid-ask half-spread, SEC §31 fee, FINRA TAF on sells; latency-induced slippage modeled. |
| **Information Ratio against an arbitrary benchmark** | Benchmark choice is a free parameter (Goetzmann et al. 2007); closet indexing (Cremers-Petajisto 2009) explodes IR. | **Appraisal Ratio** (residual α / σ_residual) or **Sharpe of long-short hedged book**; report IR vs multiple benchmarks. |
| **Equity-curve smoothness as quality proxy** | Getmansky-Lo-Makarov JFE 2004: smoothing from illiquid/managed marks understates volatility and inflates SR by 30–65%; Asness-Krail-Liew JPM 2001 lagged-beta evidence in hedge funds. | **Lagged-beta regressions; un-smoothed returns; PSR.** |
| **VaR (especially 95%)** | Not coherent — violates subadditivity (Artzner et al. 1999); tail-blind (Acerbi-Tasche 2002); Basel III FRTB replaced with 97.5% ES. | **ES_0.975** (the BCBS d352 / FRTB standard). |
| **Sharpe from short backtests without MBL adjustment** | With N trials on T years, expected max SR under H₀ ≈ √(2 ln N / T); 5-year daily backtest with N=7 silent trials guarantees IS Sharpe ≈ 1 under pure noise (Bailey-Borwein-LdP-Zhu 2014). | **MBL formula: T_years ≥ 2 ln(N) / SR²**; reject if backtest is shorter. |
| **Win/loss streak counts as edge evidence** | Pure runs-test statistical artifact (Wald-Wolfowitz 1940; Mood 1940); distribution of streaks under i.i.d. with given p produces "impressive" streaks routinely. | **Bootstrap streak distribution under permutation null** and compute the actual percentile. |
| **Sortino as primary metric without MAR and divisor disclosure** | Goetzmann et al. 2007 lists it among gameable measures; MAR is arbitrary; the all-N vs only-losses-N divisor difference produces wildly different numbers. | **State MAR and divisor; pair with Sharpe;** use for asymmetric strategies only. |
| **Calmar without time normalization** | MDD scales as √T (μ=0), log T (μ>0), or T (μ<0); naive comparison across track records of different length is invalid. | **Magdon-Ismail E[MDD]-scaled Calmar**, or Ulcer Performance Index. |
| **Excess kurtosis as a point-estimate decision input** | Sample γ₂ is not consistent under heavy tails; dominated by single observations (Cohen-Davis-Samorodnitsky 2020). | **Student-t fit with ν d.o.f. + ES**, or Hill estimator for tail index. |
| **Beta as a single rolling number** | Single-factor; bull/bear asymmetry hidden (Ang-Chen-Xing 2006). | **β_up vs β_down separately**; multi-factor Fama-French-Carhart. |
| **Pearson correlation matrix below T ≥ 10·N²** | Marchenko-Pastur noise dominates eigenvalues; inverse used in optimization is hyper-sensitive. | **Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage; random-matrix filtering; pair with conditional ρ in worst-decile days.** |

# Closing notes

**The single most underrated metric in retail trading is the gap between in-sample DSR and the first 6 months of live PSR.** Traders who pre-commit to a kill threshold here outperform those who don't by a margin that swamps every other refinement on this page. The most overrated metric is raw Sharpe, which retail content treats as a noun rather than an estimator with a confidence interval; the second most overrated is win rate, which is a parameter of stop placement masquerading as a property of the strategy. **The dominant epistemic risk in retail backtesting is multiple-testing-induced false discovery** — formalized as backtest overfitting (Bailey-Borwein-LdP-Zhu), the factor zoo (Harvey-Liu-Zhu), and selection bias under the maximum-order-statistic of N noisy Sharpe ratios. With modern compute, a retail researcher routinely tries thousands of variants on a fixed price history but reports the best. The expected maximum in-sample Sharpe under a pure-noise null grows like √(2 ln N / T); on 5 years of daily data and 1,000 silent trials, a Sharpe of ~1.3 is essentially guaranteed at zero true edge. Every other failure mode in this report is either a *mechanism* by which researchers (consciously or not) generate additional implicit trials, or a *symptom* of having selected the lucky variant. The defensible response is procedural rather than mathematical: pre-register the strategy, record every trial, apply DSR / PBO / multiple-testing-adjusted t-thresholds (≥ 3.0 per HLZ), demand realistic post-cost performance (Novy-Marx-Velikov), and treat any retail backtest reporting Sharpe > 2 without disclosure of the number of trials as overfit until proven otherwise.
