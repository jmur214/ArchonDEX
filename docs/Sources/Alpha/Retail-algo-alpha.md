# An honest map of retail algo alpha in 2026

**Bottom line up front:** Genuine, durable retail algo alpha exists, but the menu is narrower and less glamorous than retail-trader media suggests. The structurally cleanest opportunities in 2026 cluster in three places: (1) **capacity-bounded niches** institutional capital is mandate-blocked from — microcap mean-reversion, niche commodity futures, illiquid ETFs, closed-end fund discounts; (2) **event-driven trades with clear forced counterparties** — insider Form 4 cluster buys, hand-curated merger arb, small-cap spinoffs, earnings volatility crush; (3) **systematic risk-premium harvesting with materially better drawdown shape than SPY** — managed-futures trend, cash-secured put-write. Most of the popular factor zoo (low-vol, quality, large-cap momentum) is now SPY-with-a-tilt, not alpha. Most of the popular options content (0DTE, dispersion at retail scale, naked short-vol) ranges from break-even to wealth-destructive after honest cost modeling. McLean & Pontiff's 58% post-publication decay and Chen & Velikov's 93%-after-cost decay rate are the right priors: assume any published factor is mostly arbitraged unless you can name *who must keep providing the trade and why they cannot stop*.

This dossier is opinionated by design. Numbers are sourced where given; reasoning-from-priors is labeled `[Reasoning]`.

# PART 1 — Research Framework

## 1. Where retail-accessible inefficiencies actually come from

The starting empirical fact is McLean & Pontiff (2016, *JF* 71(1)): of 97 documented predictors, returns decay **~26% out-of-sample and ~58% post-publication**. Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016, *RFS* 29(1)) argue a credible new factor needs **t > 3.0**, not 2.0. Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020, *RFS* 33(5)) find **65% of 452 anomalies fail at t=1.96 with NYSE breakpoints and value weights, 82% fail at t=2.78**. Crucially, the anomalies that survive only in microcaps are exactly the ones an institution cannot scale into — a structural gift to retail.

**Institutional mandate constraints.** Active mutual funds and pensions are paid on info ratio versus a benchmark, not absolute return. Tracking-error budgets, market-cap minimums (S&P 500 needs >$22.7B in 2026; Russell 1000 floor ~$3B), index-inclusion rules, and ESG carve-outs all force price-insensitive flow. **Who's on the other side of the trade:** indexed and benchmark-hugging capital that must transact regardless of price. **Why they keep providing it:** fiduciary mandate language and career risk. PMs cannot deviate ±400 bps from benchmark; they cannot put 8% in a $300M-cap name. The McLean-Pontiff result that **post-publication decay is weaker in stocks with high idiosyncratic risk and low liquidity** is direct evidence — institutional arbitrage capital is mandate-blocked from the microcap tail.

**Regulatory carve-outs.** Volcker (2014) and Basel III LCR/NSFR shrank bank dealer risk-warehousing in HY credit, structured products, and equity prop, leaving residual liquidity demand. Reg NMS asymmetry between SIP and direct feeds is a *retail disadvantage* on sub-millisecond timing — call this out honestly. **The relevant 2026 regulatory change: the SEC approved elimination of the $25K PDT rule on April 14, 2026, effective June 4, 2026**, replaced with intraday margin standards (SEC/FINRA Rule 4210 amendments) — this materially loosens retail equity friction this year.

**Capacity limits.** This is the **single biggest retail edge**. A strategy whose alpha caps at $50M is uninvestable for a hedge fund (fee economics fail) but lucrative for a retail trader. Microcap mean-reversion, niche commodity carry, single-name vol-RV in tickers with <500 OI at ATM, small-ETF NAV plays — all live here. Institutional capital is *structurally* absent, not arbitraged-away.

**Information asymmetries.** Sell-side coverage on a $300M-cap name is 0–3 analysts versus ~22 on a S&P 500 name. Slower diffusion → tradable post-earnings drift (Hong-Lim-Stein 2000 documents PEAD persistence specifically in low-coverage names). Institutional research approval cycles (IC → compliance → staged entry) run 2–6 weeks; retail can be in and out before the IC meets.

**Behavioral biases (durable because wired).** Disposition effect (Odean 1998) produces short-term momentum in winners and reversal in losers. Anchoring to 52-week highs (George & Hwang 2004) still generates a tradable post-anchor drift. The MAX/lottery preference (Bali-Cakici-Whitelaw 2011) creates a persistent negative-alpha tilt on the short side. **Post-Robinhood attention-driven flow** (Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz 2022) generates 2–5 day reversals in heavily-bought names; the BJZZ retail-flow algorithm (Boehmer, Jones, Zhang & Zhang 2021, *JF* 76(5)) shows retail order imbalance positively predicts next-week returns at ~3 bps/day — though Barber, Hu, Jones, Odean & Zhao (2023) document weakening since 2016 and especially post-2020. **Who's on the other side and why they persist:** the retail traders themselves are the providers; they keep providing because the biases are hard-wired psychology, not learnable mistakes.

**Microstructure quirks.** Closing-auction imbalances (now ~10–11% of daily volume vs 3.1% in 2010); options market-maker hedging flows producing net-short-gamma reflexive amplification and net-long-gamma vol suppression; ETF iNAV discrepancies during create/redeem windows on small or exotic ETFs; and event-driven gamma flips around monthly OPEX. **Who provides:** liquidity-demanding rebalancers and option-MMs constrained on inventory. **Why it persists:** mechanical mandate flow doesn't compete on price.

## 2. Retail-vs-institutional asymmetry — honest version

**Real retail advantages:** sub-institutional sizing (the #1 edge — you can deploy $50K in a $200M-cap stock without moving the print); no quarterly career risk and no redemption windows (can run a 3-year drawdown); no tracking-error budget; no fund-expense drag (a 6% gross strategy nets 6% to retail vs ~3% to a hedge-fund LP); no benchmark-hugging pressure; access to capacity-limited niches; June 2026 PDT elimination. Barber & Odean's famous 6.5%/yr underperformance of active retail (1991–96, *JF* 55(2)) is the *aggregate* result for unsystematic behaviorally-biased trading — a disciplined retail algo is not the average.

**Real retail disadvantages.** Data quality (Bloomberg/Refinitiv at $24K+/yr/seat covers PIT fundamentals and restated history that retail substitutes only approximate). Execution and PFOF — the Levy (2022, Wharton) randomized study showed Susquehanna paid TDA $0.10/100sh with mid-price execution but paid Robinhood $0.75/100sh with zero price improvement; the 2020 SEC enforcement cited Robinhood execution losses of $5/order on 100+ share trades and $15 on 500+ share trades. **For algo retail running marketable orders >100 shares, use IBKR Pro SmartRouting or Fidelity Direct, not Alpaca's broker-internalized routing.** Financing rates: retail margin runs 5.5–12% across Alpaca/IBKR/Robinhood vs institutional SOFR + 25–75 bps (~5.0–5.6%) — a 2–6% annualized headwind on 2x-levered carry. No prime-brokerage shorting/locates at scale; capped securities-lending revenue capture (~15–50% of spread vs institutional ~80%). Tax drag at high turnover (short-term gains at up to 37% federal). No IPO allocations worth getting. Talent and data-density gap — a single Citadel pod has 30+ researchers with alt-data; solo retail has self plus open source.

## 3. Where to look — markets and instruments favoring retail

**Microcap and nanocap equities ($50M–$500M cap).** Below ~$500M cap institutional ownership thins; below ~$200M coverage is 0–2 analysts; below ~$100M most mandates exclude entirely. Hou-Xue-Zhang's "trading frictions" anomalies that fail at NYSE breakpoints **succeed in microcaps because the binding factor is capital constraint, not signal degradation**. Tradable angles: PEAD (Garfinkel/Hribar/Hsiao 2024 still find ~5.1% 3-month risk-adjusted return on long top-SUE / short bottom-SUE microcaps), insider buying clusters, "Lazy Prices" 10-K language change (Cohen-Malloy-Nguyen 2020 documented 22% alpha to minimal-change filers). Slippage 50–100 bps round-trip is the binding constraint; position sizing at ≤2% of 20-day ADV is non-negotiable.

**Small-cap options surfaces.** Single-name options on $200M–$2B caps often have OI <500 per ATM strike and bid-ask >5% of premium; MM vol-surface models are simpler. Tradable angles: earnings-vol crush (sell straddles when IV rank >80% — known capacity-limited edge), term-structure dislocations between weeklies and monthlies, skew arb in names with directional retail call demand.

**Niche commodity futures.** Cocoa (CC), Lumber (LBR/MLBR), Oats (ZO), Feeder Cattle (GF), Orange Juice (OJ), Rough Rice (ZR) have OI 10K–100K — dedicated commodity funds focus on liquid energy/metals. Carver's *Advanced Futures Trading Strategies* (2023) is the playbook. Retail single-direction capacity in cocoa is maybe $5–20M before slippage degrades — well inside retail territory.

**Event-driven situations.** M&A (announced cash-deal spreads at 3–6% IRR; break risk pricing inefficient in <$2B deals); spinoffs (forced selling by index funds); index reconstitution (Russell June, S&P quarterly — but compression is severe, see Part 2); SPAC redemption arb (mostly dead).

**ETF arbitrage mechanics.** Small/exotic ETFs (single-country EMs, thematic, leveraged/inverse, niche bond) can trade 50–200 bps from iNAV during stress. Retail cannot create/redeem, but can take the other side of converging NAV gaps where AP attention is weak. Petajisto (2017, *FAJ*) documented mean ETF premium 6 bps but time-series volatility 49 bps; long-short relative-mispricing strategy gross IR ~2 at $100M.

**Single-stock volatility.** Idiosyncratic vol pricing in 2,000+ optionable names is uneven; MMs use vol-of-vol heuristics. Euan Sinclair's *Volatility Trading* (2nd ed., 2013) and *Positional Option Trading* (2020) remain the canonical retail-accessible playbooks.

**Fixed-income relative value — mostly NOT retail-feasible.** Cash bonds trade OTC; no retail repo access; swap-spread/asset-swap/cross-currency-basis trades require prime-broker cross-margining. Treasury futures basis specifically is hedge-fund territory only (gross spread 5–15 bps; retail financing 5%+; needs 20–50x leverage you cannot get). Express duration views via TLT/IEF/SHY ETFs and ZN/ZB/ZF futures — fine. True RV — skip.

**Crypto microstructure.** Perp funding rates and cash-and-carry basis are accessible via IBKR (IBIT spot + /MBT CME futures) or via direct exchange APIs. Cross-exchange arb mostly closed (capital controls bind retail), but funding-rate capture remains tradable when basis > financing cost + 200 bps.

## 4. Research process — avoiding self-deception

**Hypothesis generation.** A structural hypothesis ("microcap PEAD is slower because coverage is sparse — form before testing") survives McLean-Pontiff decay better than a regression-derived signal. Harvey-Liu-Zhu's prescription: assume your discovery is one of 1,000+ tested; require t > 3.0 minimum. Pure data mining requires even higher hurdles.

**Backtest pitfalls.** Survivorship bias is the dominant trap in microcap work — CRSP delisting returns (-30% to -100% on failures) must be included; Yahoo/Google Finance silently inflate microcap backtests by 2–5% annualized. Use Norgate, Sharadar SF1, or CRSP Standard for PIT membership. Look-ahead bias appears in restated financials and earnings dates pegged to fiscal-period end instead of announcement date. Data snooping and multiple testing — Harvey & Liu (2014, *JPM*, "Evaluating Trading Strategies") propose Sharpe haircuts based on number of trials; with 100 backtests an apparent Sharpe of 1.5 deflates to ~0.6.

**Marcos López de Prado's toolkit is non-optional.** Current affiliations (verified 2026): Global Head of Quantitative R&D at ADIA; Professor of Practice at Cornell Engineering; founding board member ADIA Lab. Required reading and tools: **Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (Bailey & López de Prado 2014, *JPM* 40(5); SSRN 2460551) — corrects observed SR for selection bias and non-normality; if you ran 50 backtests and the best is SR=1.8, the DSR is typically <1.0. **Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio and Minimum Track Record Length** — same authors. **Probability of Backtest Overfitting** via **Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation** (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2014, *Notices of the AMS* 61(5); SSRN 2308659; Python `pypbo`). **Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation** for path-dependent labels. **Triple-Barrier Method + Meta-Labeling.** **Hierarchical Risk Parity.** Books: *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (Wiley 2018), *Machine Learning for Asset Managers* (Cambridge 2020) — the better entry point — and *Causal Factor Investing* (Cambridge 2023).

**Walk-forward analysis.** Use expanding window when parameters are stable; rolling window when regime change is the binding concern (most realistic for 2010–2026). K-fold walk-forward with 1–2 week embargo prevents label leakage on horizons >1 day.

**Transaction cost realism.** Commissions are effectively zero on Alpaca/IBKR Lite. Real costs that dominate: half-spread crossing (5–50 bps in microcaps, <1 bp in SPY), market impact (square-root law: impact ≈ σ × √(Q/ADV)), opportunity cost of unfilled limits, locate fees on shorts, regulatory fees. **Rule of thumb:** if your backtest shows SR > 2 and depends on round-trip cost <10 bps, redo at 25 bps before believing it. For microcaps, assume 50–100 bps round-trip minimum.

**Regime fragility.** Mandatory stress tests: 2008 GFC; Q4 2018 volmageddon; March 2020 COVID; 2022 bond-equity correlation flip; 2023 SVB; August 5, 2024 yen carry unwind. If a strategy doesn't survive 3+ of these intact, regime fragility is the binding risk.

**Paper trading discipline.** [Reasoning] Minimum 3 months paper after every strategy iteration; minimum 6 months live at 10–25% of intended capital before scaling. Track expected vs realized slippage explicitly — if realized > expected by >50%, halt and revisit cost model. Paper reveals execution/operational issues but does not reveal psychology under real PnL; small-live is the critical phase.

**Recent counter-evidence to keep on file.** Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023, *JF*) argue the factor zoo holds up under Bayesian multiple-testing if cross-factor correlations are modeled — less pessimistic than Hou-Xue-Zhang. Chen & Zimmermann (2022, *CFR*) find higher reproduction rates with consistent methodology. Duarte, Jones, Khorram, Mo & Wang (2025) show **equity-option factor replications inflate Sharpe by 1000%+ due to ex-post filtering**.

## Category A — Capacity-bounded equity edges (the cleanest retail alpha)

### A1. Microcap mean-reversion with regime filter

**Thesis.** Buy microcaps ($50M–$500M cap) on short-term oversold conditions (5-day RSI < 10 or 5-day low) while the broad regime is favorable (SPY > 200-DMA); hold 3–10 days. **Mechanism:** forced selling by tax-loss harvesters, margin-called retail, redemption-driven small-cap mutual funds, and inventory-laying market makers — combined with thin institutional arb capital because the names are below mandate cutoffs. **Who's on the other side and why they persist:** retail panic sellers, mutual-fund redemption flow, and MM inventory — none of these can stop providing because the flow is mechanically driven by external pressure. **Capacity:** $1–10M for a true-nanocap book; $10–50M for a microcap-only book — retail $5k–$100k is deep inside capacity. **Implementation:** Norgate Data ($80/mo, survivorship-bias-free) is essentially required; IBKR for borrow if shorting (don't); limit orders only; position ≤2% of 20-day ADV per name; 30–50 name basket. **Profile:** Quantitativo's 2024 RSI(2) replication: Sharpe 1.14, CAGR 25.7% vs NDX 17.6% (2010–2024); long-only microcap mean-reversion typically Sharpe 0.8–1.2 net of ~30 bps round-trip, max DD 25–35%. **Counter-thesis:** Hou-Xue-Zhang argue most microcap anomalies *are* cost compensation, not alpha — once you cross spreads and pay slippage on illiquid names, the gross edge collapses. Survivorship bias inflates retail backtests dramatically. March 2020 microcap drawdown was -45%. **Verdict:** **Real, retail-feasible, the cleanest standalone alpha on this menu** — but only with disciplined cost modeling, ADV-based sizing, survivorship-free data, and ≥30 names. Avoid sub-$50M caps unless deeply experienced.

### A2. Insider buying clusters (Form 4)

**Thesis.** Open-market purchases by officers/directors using personal capital are informed; cluster buys (3+ insiders within 30 days) in small-caps are the strongest variant. **Mechanism:** information asymmetry that survives because filings hit EDGAR within 2 business days but small-cap algos cannot trade them economically given borrow/slippage. **Who's on the other side:** slower-adjusting retail and institutional holders. **Capacity:** $20–200M strategy-wide; retail trivially uncapped. **Implementation:** EDGAR Form 4 is free and XBRL-tagged; OpenInsider aggregates; filter routines from Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (2012, *JF*) — exclude option exercises, exclude 10b5-1 plan trades, focus on CEO/CFO, weight $-amount relative to comp. IBKR for small-cap execution; Alpaca fine for liquid names. **Profile:** Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski (2012) opportunistic insider long-short generated VW abnormal returns of 82 bps/month (~9.8% annualized, t=2.15) and EW 180 bps/month (~21.6%, t=6.07); routine traders ~0 alpha. Lakonishok-Lee (2001) baseline: small-cap insider buys ~7.4% abnormal 12-mo. Zhao (2025) microcap ML overlay: AUC 0.70 OOS 2024, mean CAR 6.3% in top bucket. **Counter-thesis:** 20+ years of mining; HFTs front-run filings within minutes; SEC's December 2022 10b5-1 reform shortened plan cooling-off periods (complicates classification); some AUC degradation 2023→2024. **Verdict:** **Alive in small/microcap.** Best risk-adjusted free-data event-driven strategy in this dossier. Expect 8–15% gross, Sharpe 0.8–1.2 net, holding 6–12 months. Underweight large-caps where the signal is fully arbitraged.

### A3. Closed-end fund discount + Saba piggyback

**Thesis.** CEFs trade at persistent discounts to NAV that mean-revert; muni CEFs entered 2026 at ~13.6% average discount (vs ~4% historical) — the widest in 15 years — and Saba/Boaz Weinstein's activist campaigns provide the catalyst. **Mechanism:** retired retail income-seekers panic-sell during rate spikes (widening discounts); activist 13D filings force tenders, conversions, or open-endings that close the gap. **Who's on the other side:** retired retail and the original IPO subscribers paying premium; the limits-to-arbitrage rents persist because absolute return investors don't dominate the CEF holder base. **Capacity:** total US CEF market ~$250B; strategy capacity ~$1–5B before crowding. **Implementation:** CEFConnect (free) for daily NAV/premium; track z-score vs 1Y rolling mean; buy at z > 1.5σ wide with reasonable NAV trajectory; hedge underlying duration/equity with sector ETFs; piggyback Saba 13D filings (free on EDGAR). Hold 6–18 months. **Profile:** CUNY paper BMR strategy: in-sample Sharpe 1.86; OOS 1998–2011 Q5-Q1 quintile spread economically significant. Saba CEFS ETF has outperformed PCEF passive index. Sharpe 0.9–1.3 net realistic. Max DD 10–15% in rate/credit shocks (discounts widen exactly when NAV falls — double-whammy). **Counter-thesis:** discounts can persist for years (Lee-Shleifer-Thaler 1991 "limits to arbitrage"); NYSE/Cboe filed 2024 to eliminate annual meeting requirement at SEC's behest — would crush activist leverage; you're effectively levered into bonds with negative carry on the hedge. **Verdict:** **Alive and arguably the most underappreciated retail event-driven trade going into 2026.** Free EDGAR data, clear catalyst calendar via Saba filings, multi-year-wide setup. Regulatory tail risk is the binding concern.

### A4. Hand-curated merger arbitrage

**Thesis.** Announced cash-deal targets trade at spreads to deal price; 90%+ of announced cash deals complete; long-term holders exit at announcement, arbitrageurs provide certainty for spread compensation. **Mechanism:** Mitchell & Pulvino (2001, *JF*) — arb returns are uncorrelated with the market in normal times but correlated in deep drawdowns; it's an insurance-sale strategy. **Who's on the other side:** long-term holders exiting; sellers of deal-break insurance. **Capacity:** strategy-wide $50–100B (Verdad estimates); single-deal retail is a price-taker, but a 5–15 position book at $5k–$100k is the minimum viable diversification. **Implementation:** EDGAR free for 8-K announcements and DEFM14A merger proxies; InsideArbitrage and MergerArbitrageLimited aggregate; IBKR for execution (some foreign-listed; stock-for-stock needs short of acquirer); monitor HSR clearance, FTC/DOJ commentary, EU CMA, China SAMR, vote dates. **Profile:** HFRI Merger Arb Index ~6–7% annualized, 3–4% vol, **Sharpe 1.0–1.5** historically. Klymochko (Sep 2024) cites 6.7% return, 3.2% vol, Sharpe 1.46 over 25 years. AllianceBernstein Nov 2025 notes Q3 2025 US deals >$5B up 166% YoY with friendlier regulatory backdrop. **Counter-thesis:** crowded — Rzakhanov & Jetley (2019) show diseconomies of scale; ETF wrappers (MNA, ARB, CSMA) drag generic alpha; retail can't access pre-announcement leak trades; negative skew (Activision/Adobe-Figma type breaks erase years). **Verdict:** **Alive and useful as a diversifier.** Hand-curated 8–12 deal book on IBKR realistically delivers 6–10% net at Sharpe 0.8–1.2 with very low SPY correlation (0.2–0.4 ex-crisis). Skip stock-for-stock unless comfortable shorting acquirer. Avoid the widest spreads (priced for failure). If unwilling to source deals, MNA/CSMA gives 80% of exposure with no work — but you surrender ~2% of the Sharpe edge.

### A5. Small-cap spinoffs (Greenblatt filter)

**Thesis.** Spun-off subsidiaries outperform because index funds and large-cap mandates force-sell the small unknown stubs; standalone focus and improved management incentives unlock value. **Mechanism:** forced selling at distribution (Cusatis-Miles-Woolridge 1993). **Capacity:** small — each event is sub-billion in float typically. **Implementation:** EDGAR Form 10/10-12B (free); stockspinoffs.com tracking; IBKR or Alpaca handle the auto-distribution. Hold 6–36 months. **Profile:** Cusatis et al. — spinoffs +30% over 3 years vs benchmark, parents +18%. Veld-Veld-Merkoulova (2009) meta-review confirms ~1–3% announcement-day return; long-run persistence "mixed." Sharpe 0.6–0.9 with small N and event lumpiness. **Counter-thesis:** McConnell, Ozbilgin & Wahal (2001) showed the strategy does NOT beat Fama-French 3-factor or matched-firm benchmarks out-of-sample — the spinoff alpha may not survive proper benchmarking. Modern passive flow may have eliminated forced-sale dynamics. **SPARC status:** Pershing Square SPARC Holdings still no deal executed as of May 2026 (Ackman's December 2025 SpaceX-merger-via-SPARC pitch on X remains speculative posturing). Effectively a one-off Ackman vehicle; not investable as a strategy. **Verdict:** **Alive but selective.** Don't index spinoffs (CSD ETF returns are mediocre). Focus on (a) <$2B float, (b) where parent dumps non-core/ugly business (classic Greenblatt setup), (c) heavy insider buying post-spin. Expect Sharpe 0.7–1.0, lumpy, 12–24 month holds.

### A6. ETF NAV-discrepancy plays (Petajisto-style)

**Thesis.** Retail cannot do true creation/redemption arbitrage, but can opportunistically trade relative mispricings between similar ETFs and post-stale-NAV reversal in illiquid international/bond/niche ETFs. **Mechanism:** Petajisto (2017, *FAJ*) — mean ETF premium 6 bps but time-series volatility 49 bps (40 bps VW); equal-weight 95% range ±96 bps. APs constrained at quarter-end and in bond ETFs (Pan-Zeng on AP dual-role conflicts). **Capacity:** Petajisto explicitly $100M long-short book → IR ≈ 2; retail is deep inside capacity, but only a handful of trades a year. **Implementation:** iNAV feeds from issuer sites (free); daily premium/discount scans on illiquid international single-country, niche bond (HY, EM debt, BKLN), commodity ETFs during market closures. IBKR for tight spreads on the illiquid leg. **Profile:** Petajisto IR ~3–4 small-scale; net Sharpe ~0.7–1.2 episodic for retail-feasible subset; max DD ~10–15%; holding 1–10 days. Pan-Zeng bond ETF stress dislocations (LQD, HYG March 2020 premiums went to -5%, snapped back in 2 weeks) — Sharpe undefined but very high during the episode. **Counter-thesis:** without AP access you're not arbitraging — you're betting on stat mean-reversion in premium with no convergence force you control. APs collapse the gap; you're along for the ride. **Verdict:** **Niche yes — opportunistic overlay during stress periods (1–3 episodes/yr) on illiquid international single-country and HY bond ETFs.** Hard to systematize daily; diversification value > standalone alpha.

## Category B — Volatility and options

### B1. Earnings volatility crush (best retail options strategy)

**Thesis.** IV systematically inflates 5–15 days into earnings and crushes 30–50% overnight; selling premium pre-EA and closing morning after captures the variance-risk premium concentrated around the announcement. **Mechanism:** Dubinsky, Johannes, Kaeck & Seeger (2019, *RFS*) formally model the earnings-jump component; straddle returns more negative during EA windows due to jump risk premium. **Who's on the other side:** retail directional bettors buying straddles to "play earnings" and informed news traders; institutional vol-arbs warehouse the premium. **Capacity:** per-name $100k–$1M per event; across 100+ earnings/quarter, several million scalable. **Implementation:** ORATS ($100/mo) is essentially designed for this — provides earnings calendar plus historical IV/realized-move statistics. Sell ATM iron condors 1–2 days before earnings, close morning after; signal IV rank > 70 plus historical RV < expected move. IBKR or Alpaca Level 3 (launched Dec 2024) for iron condors. **Profile:** practitioner backtests (Option Alpha, Volatility Box, TastyTrade) on iron condors at expected-move strikes show ~65–72% win rate, slightly positive expectancy after costs, Sharpe 0.5–0.8 net at modest size; degrades fast with size due to single-name capacity. 5–8% of events produce 2–3x expected moves (NFLX 2022, META 2022, NVDA 2024) and wipe months of premium — severe negative skew. **Counter-thesis:** diversification across uncorrelated events is the only path; bid-ask on single-name options is often 5–10%; selection criteria are easy to overfit. **Verdict:** **Genuine retail opportunity, modest Sharpe, qualifies on diversification.** Best-fit options strategy in this dossier for a Python-literate trader: signal-driven, defined-risk, 5–15 events/week. Use ORATS as your moat.

### B2. Put-write systematic (PUTW or DIY cash-secured SPY/XSP)

**Thesis.** Selling cash-secured ATM index puts monthly captures the variance risk premium with a cleaner wrapper than VIX futures. **Mechanism:** put-call parity equivalence to covered-call but T-bill collateral earns risk-free rate cleanly. Bondarenko (2019, SSRN 3393940) reports CBOE PUT Index Sharpe 0.65 vs SPX 0.49 (1986–2018), max DD -32.7% vs SPX -50.9%. **Who's on the other side:** portfolio insurance buyers, institutional hedgers, reverse-convertible structured-product issuers. **Capacity:** multi-billion; retail uncapped. **Implementation:** PUTW WisdomTree ETF (0.44% ER) or DIY weekly/monthly SPY/XSP cash-secured puts on IBKR; XSP gives Section 1256 60/40 tax treatment. Cash-secured needs full notional; credit put spreads need only width differential. **Profile:** CBOE PUT factsheet (Jan 2007 through Jan 2026): 7.0% return, 10.9% vol, Sharpe 0.50, max DD -32.7%. WPUT (weekly) max DD -24.2% — improvement over PUT. Beta to SPX ~0.6. **Counter-thesis:** Israelov decomposition shows PUT's "alpha" is mostly equity beta + VRP — both compensated risks, not skill. After short-term-gain taxation, retail PUT often underperforms simply holding SPY. Feb '20 PUT was -20% in a month. **Verdict:** **Qualifies on diversification + drawdown grounds, NOT pure Sharpe alpha.** Honest framing: a *better-shaped equity exposure*, not alpha. Most realistic path to Sharpe ≥0.8: blend with intermediate Treasuries and lever to equity vol.

### B3. Single-stock options skew / term-structure (decayed but tradable)

**Thesis.** Cross-sectional option-implied measures (IV skew, IV-spread, call-put IV difference) predict subsequent equity returns because informed traders migrate to options first. **Mechanism:** Xing, Zhang & Zhao (2010, *JFQA*) — steepest-smirk decile underperforms least-smirk decile by 10.9%/year risk-adjusted. An, Ang, Bali & Cakici (2014, *JF*) — rising past-month call IV predicts +1%/month equity returns. **Capacity:** mid/large cap; ~$50–500M institutional ceiling. **Implementation:** ORATS basic ($100/mo) or Polygon options ($79–199/mo); equity-leg portfolio rebalanced weekly/monthly; IBKR for shorts. **Profile:** original signal has substantially decayed; Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet (2022, *JF*) attribute much of the IV-skew anomaly to a proxy for stock borrow fees — implying the alpha partly compensates for short-side frictions. Zhan, Han, Cao & Tong (2022, *RFS*) — net Sharpe 0.2–0.5 after bid-ask. **Verdict:** **Plausible but decayed alpha; mid-cap focus where institutional arb is thinner.** Realistic net Sharpe 0.4–0.7 on a 50-name diversified book. Treat published numbers as upper bounds.

### B4. Volatility risk premium harvesting (with hard discipline)

**Thesis.** Implied vol exceeds realized vol ~85% of months; the premium is real but it's *compensation for tail risk, not alpha.* **Mechanism:** Eraker & Wu (2017, *JFE*) explain negative VIX-futures returns within a representative-agent equilibrium — the carry is exactly what theory predicts. **Implementation:** SPX short put spreads at 10–20Δ (best retail fit), capped SVXY/SVIX notional ≤5–10% of portfolio. **Tails are real and recurrent:** XIV -96% Feb 5, 2018; SVIX ~-59% in April 2025 tariff shock (VIX intraday 60.13 on 4/7/25); SVIX/SVXY ~-30% August 5, 2024 (VIX intraday 65.73). **Verdict:** **Conditional yes — but defined-risk only.** Sharpe 0.5–0.8 reflects fair compensation for left-tail wipeouts. Anyone marketing naked short-vol Sharpe >1.5 is hiding the tails or cherry-picking pre-2018 backtests.

### B5. Covered-call systematic (BXMD / JEPI / QYLD) — NOT alpha

**Thesis (skeptic's framing).** Covered calls decompose (Israelov & Nielsen 2015, *FAJ*, "Covered Calls Uncovered") into ~70% passive equity beta, ~10% short-vol risk premium, and ~25% **uncompensated equity-reversal exposure**. In an asymmetric right-skewed market, selling the upside is value-destructive for accumulators. **Profile:** BXMD (30Δ OTM) 1986–Feb 2026: 10.6% return, 12.9% vol, Sharpe 0.60, max DD -42.7% vs SPX TR 11.1%, 15.2% vol, Sharpe 0.56, max DD -50.9% — small Sharpe edge. QYLD: 5-yr CAGR ~7–8% vs QQQ 17–18% — massive bull-market underperformance. JEPI/QYLD distribute as ordinary income, not qualified divs — material tax drag in taxable accounts. **Verdict:** **Does NOT qualify as alpha for accumulators.** A risk-shape modifier for retirees who'd liquidate anyway. If implemented at all, DIY 30–40Δ OTM weekly SPY calls — not ATM, not QYLD. The honest framing: this is selling tails for premium, not finding mispricings.

### B6. Dispersion at retail scale — infeasible

**Thesis.** Index implied vol > sqrt(weighted single-stock IV × correlation) is priced because investors pay for diversification breakdown insurance. **Why it fails for retail:** a proper SPX dispersion book needs 50–100+ single-stock option legs; minimum $250k *before* index leg; bid-ask on single-name OTM options 5–15%; Driessen-Maenhout-Vilkov (2009, *JF*) themselves note "the correlation risk premium cannot be exploited with realistic trading frictions." Retail is competing against Optiver/SIG/Citadel desks for whom this is core P&L. **Verdict:** **DOES NOT QUALIFY for $5k–$100k accounts.** Skip unless $250k+ with portfolio margin and OptionMetrics-quality data. If you must dabble, use the Cboe DSPX index to *time* a simple short-SPX/long-QQQ vol pair at expensive-dispersion peaks.

### B7. 0DTE options — high skepticism, mostly negative-EV

**Thesis (skeptic's read).** 0DTE is >50% of SPX volume in 2024–25 (Cboe), but retail directional 0DTE buying is documented loss-making. Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya (2023) — retail options traders lost ~$2.1B aggregate Nov 2019–June 2021. Bogousslavsky & Muravyev (2025) — mean retail option trade -0.93%; **0DTE trades lose an additional -2.95% to -4.7%** vs other option trades (t=-10). Naked option *sales* are +20–28% incremental (but only ~13% of retail flow). Brogaard, Han & Won (2023) — 1-SD increase in 0DTE volume → 9.1% increase in volatility, driven by speculative retail. Cboe's own data (Mandy Xu, "Much Ado About 0DTEs") shows MM net gamma is ~0.04–0.17% of SPX daily liquidity — the "gamma squeeze edge" retail thinks they have is largely myth. **Implementation reality:** sub-$25k accounts pre-June-2026 were PDT-blocked from running this; even post-PDT-elimination, SPX 1256 iron condors entered post-9:45am, held ≤2 hrs, 1–2% account risk max is the only disciplined expression. **Verdict:** **Retail has NO structural edge in 0DTE.** A disciplined defined-risk premium-selling program can extract Sharpe 0.4–0.8 best case; the median retail 0DTE trader is providing liquidity and lottery-ticket premium to Citadel/Susquehanna/Optiver. Pick your battles.

## Category C — Cross-asset and systematic

### C1. Managed-futures trend following (best portfolio diversifier)

**Thesis.** Each asset's own 1/3/12-month past return predicts next-month direction; combined across 50+ futures markets with vol-targeting, you get genuine "crisis alpha" and diversification to equity beta. **Mechanism:** Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012, *JFE*) — significant TSMOM in 58 of 58 futures contracts. Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen (2017, *JPM*, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing") find positive returns every decade since 1880, net Sharpe ~0.4 across 67 markets net of costs. **Who's on the other side:** hedgers (commercials offloading price risk), trend-fading discretionary traders, rebalancing pensions. Persistent because hedging demand is structural. **Capacity:** industry $300B+, retail unlimited. **Implementation:** **Direct via micro futures on IBKR** — /MES, /MNQ, /MGC, /MCL, /M2K, /ZN micros let a $25k account run ~6-market trend system (Carver 2023 *Advanced Futures Trading Strategies* is the playbook; Alpaca does NOT offer futures). **ETF replication** — DBMF ($2.5B AUM, 0.85% ER), KMLM (0.90%), CTA Simplify (0.75%), FMF, RSST (stacked). **Profile:** AQR/Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen century: net-of-fee Sharpe 0.4–0.7, positive returns in 8 of 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns. SG CTA Index 2000–May 2024: 4.8% annualized (Morningstar). DBMF since 2019: trailing Sharpe ~0.7–0.9, max DD ~-20%. KMLM max DD -27%. **Counter-thesis:** the "crisis alpha" rests heavily on 2008 and 2022; industry has slowed (CTAs now correlate with 120-day MA models vs 60-day pre-2007); 2013–2019 was meh-to-bad; much of historical Sharpe came from the secular bond bull market 1981–2020. **Verdict:** **Real, retail-implementable, the genuine best portfolio diversifier on this menu.** Standalone Sharpe may miss 0.8, but **diversification to SPY (correlation -0.27 to -0.40 for DBMF/KMLM)** and **drawdown profile** are unambiguously real. Hold 10–25% in DBMF/KMLM as overlay, or run a 6–10 market /M-series futures system per Carver.

### C2. Commodity term structure (basis / slope)

**Thesis.** Long backwardated commodities, short contangoed ones, monthly rebalance — a genuine risk-premium / inventory-signal factor with low equity correlation. **Mechanism:** Erb & Harvey (2006, *FAJ*) — basis strategy 6.5% excess return, Sharpe ~0.85 historical. Gorton & Rouwenhorst (2006) — basis correlates with inventory; low inventory → backwardation → positive expected return. Bakshi, Gao & Rossi (2019) — slope factor distinct from carry/momentum, robust. **Who's on the other side:** hedgers (producers in backwardation forced to sell forward at discount) and passive long flows in commodity ETFs systematically eating roll loss in contango. **Implementation:** **Micro futures** — /MES, /MCL ($100/barrel), /MGC (10 oz gold), /MNG natural gas micro — accessible at $20k+ accounts; standard /CL too big for <$50k. ETFs: BCI (broad commodities, 0.25% ER, K-1 free), COMT, KRBN (carbon), BCD. Diversify across 8+ commodities. **Profile:** historical Sharpe 0.6–0.85 (Erb-Harvey, Levine-Ooi-Richardson NBER w22793); post-financialization Tang-Xiong note equities correlation rose 2008–2014, diluting diversification; recent 2021–2022 commodity boom revived edge. Sharpe 0.5–0.8 net realistic. **Verdict:** **Yes** — best implemented as 5–10% portfolio diversifier via micro-futures basis L/S basket at AUM ≥$30k or ETF basket otherwise.

### C3. Crypto cash-and-carry basis (conditional)

**Thesis.** Long spot/IBIT-FBTC plus short CME /MBT or /BTC futures (or short perp on a US-legal venue) captures the term premium / funding rate. **Mechanism:** funding-rate mechanism tethers perps to spot; futures embed risk premium for institutional ETF-substitute demand. **Profile:** 2020–2023 Sharpe 3–6 gross was real (Schmeling/Liu and arXiv 2510.14435 "Crypto as an Investable Asset Class" 2025: annualized Sharpe 6.45 for the 2020–2025 window). 2024: ~10–15% basis, Sharpe 2–4 net retail with CME route. **Q1 2025: basis collapsed to 4% — below 10-yr Treasury yield — making it negative arbitrage net of IBKR 5.5–6% financing.** 2026 currently: basis 5–9%, perp funding 8–15% annualized (Block Scholes weekly). **Implementation:** IBKR with IBIT spot + /MBT (0.1 BTC, ~$2–5k margin) short, sized small; opportunistic add when funding spikes >15% (mania regimes). **Verdict:** **Conditional yes — gated on basis > financing cost + 200 bps.** Net Sharpe 0.8–1.5 in normal regime, episodically much higher. 5–15% of crypto sleeve.

### C4. Currency carry (marginal)

**Thesis.** Long high-yield / short low-yield G10 currencies earns durable risk premium (~5–6% historical) compensating for FX/dollar-factor risk. **Mechanism:** Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan (2011, *RFS*). Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen (2008) — negative skew ("up by stairs, down by elevator"). **Profile:** DBV (Invesco G10 Currency Harvest) has been roughly flat to negative cumulative since 2008; 2024 August yen unwind hit hard. Doskov-Swinkels (2015) 1900–2012: Sharpe 0.2–0.4. Currency momentum+carry combo (Menkhoff et al. 2012): combined Sharpe ~0.8. **Verdict:** **Marginal — diversification only.** Standalone Sharpe usually <0.8; with G10 rate differentials compressed post-Fed-cuts, risk per unit of yield is highest in 30 years. If implemented, use micro futures (/6E /6J /6A /6N) carry+momentum overlay 5–10% of portfolio, not DBV (high fees, structural drag).

## Category D — Microstructure and short-horizon

### D1. Overnight drift overlay (conditional)

**Thesis.** Persistent close-to-open drift in US equity indices; long SPY/QQQ overnight, risk-off intraday. **Mechanism:** Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019, *JFE*) — momentum lives overnight, reversal intraday. Bogousslavsky (2021, *JFE*) — mispricing factor decays end-of-day as arbitrageurs unwind to avoid overnight margin. Lachance (2023, *RFE*) — one-fifth of stocks have stat-significant positive overnight bias. Closing auctions are ~10–11% of daily volume (vs 3.1% in 2010). **Profile:** Lachance/Elm-Wealth long-SPY-overnight Sharpe ~0.6–0.8 over 30 years; max DD ~15%. **But AlphaTrAI's NSPY and NIWM overnight ETFs launched July 2022 and closed Aug 2023 after 7.6% (R2K) / 1.7% (SPY) underperformance — live capture is much harder than backtest.** Daily 2x turnover frictions are real. **Implementation:** IBKR for MOC/MOO order types (Alpaca's are weaker); regime filter on VIX/term-structure improves robustness. **Counter-thesis:** Hendershott-Livdan-Rösch (2020) — night returns may compensate for asymmetric risk-bearing capacity, not be exploitable. **Verdict:** **Conditional yes** — SPY/QQQ overnight as a portfolio overlay with regime filter survives the bar on diversification grounds; expected net Sharpe 0.5–0.8. Single-name overnight is questionable. MOC same-day scalping is HFT territory — do not pursue.

### D2. Buy-the-dip (Connors-style mean-reversion on SPY/QQQ)

**Thesis.** Short-horizon reversal — buy after RSI(2) <10 or 5-day low, exit on 5-day high. Liquidity-provision premium paid by forced sellers (vol-targeters deleveraging, retail panic). **Profile:** SetupAlpha/QuantifiedStrategies replications of SPY 2000–2024: ~7–10% CAGR with ~25% time-in-market; Sharpe 0.8–1.2 *over invested days*, 0.5–0.7 full-period; large episodic losses when dip turns into crash (2008 -40%, March 2020 -15% intra-strategy). **Verdict:** **Real but small and regime-dependent.** This is a liquidity-provision premium, not pure alpha. Sharpe 0.6–0.9 with patient discipline through bear markets. Better as a timing overlay than standalone.

### D3. Pairs / cointegration stat-arb (largely dead, marginal in cross-asset)

**Thesis.** Classical Gatev-style distance pairs is dead post-2003. **Evidence:** Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst (2006, *RFS*) — 11% annualized pre-1990; Do & Faff (2010, *FAJ*) — monthly excess return collapsed from ~118 bps to ~38 bps gross post-1990 and largely negative net of costs; Rad, Low & Faff (2015) confirm decay across distance/cointegration/copula. Krauss (2017, *J Economic Surveys*): "distance dead, cointegration weak, ML research-grade." **Marginal use:** ML-enhanced or cointegration on cross-asset (gold/gold miners, oil/energy stocks, crypto majors) at Sharpe 0.5–0.8 net qualifies on diversification. **Verdict:** Don't expect alpha; expect uncorrelated mediocrity. ≤10% of book.

### D4. Calendar effects (mostly tiny, mostly mined)

**Turn-of-month (TOM):** Etula et al. (2020) propose pension cash-flow mechanism; Quantseeker 2024 SPY confirms pattern still visible 1993–2024. Sharpe 0.7–1.0 gross over invested days; ~0.5–0.8 net after costs. Real-but-tiny; mechanistic; useful as overlay timing. **Pre-FOMC drift:** Lucca & Moench (2015, *JF*) found 49 bps in 24h pre-FOMC accounting for ~80% of annual equity premium 1994–2011. **Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert (2021, *FRL*, "The Disappearing Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift"): effect essentially disappeared after 2015.** Conditional variants survive on subsets but are too low-N to validate. **Verdict: dead as published.** **Sell in May / Halloween:** Jacobsen-Zhang (2018) 323 years; Hansen SPA tests reject the trading strategy. Trivial to act on. **Santa rally:** ~30 bps total; noise net of frictions. **Overall:** mostly dead, mostly tiny.

## Category E — Factor strategies (mostly demoted to "SPY with a tilt")

### E1. Small-cap cross-sectional momentum (marginal)

12-1 long-only top decile, monthly rebalance, on small caps with a SPY-200DMA regime filter. **Profile:** Long-short large-cap 12-1 is effectively zero net Sharpe 2005–2024 (Sathish 2025, SSRN 5367656: -0.23 Sharpe, -81% DD); long leg works but short leg is uninvestable. Small-cap long-only with crash filter: Sharpe 0.5–0.7 net, drawdowns 50%+ (Daniel-Moskowitz 2016 "Momentum Crashes"). **Verdict:** **Real but tiny risk-premium for crash exposure, not free alpha.** Long-only MTUM is a tech-tilted SPY clone. Don't bother shorting.

### E2. Faber 10-month / GTAA trend (weakened)

5-asset rotation by 10-month MA; Faber 1973–2012 — 10.5% CAGR / -10% max DD. **2013–2024 replications (CXO Advisory, QuantifiedStrategies, Extrategic Dashboard) show degraded performance — roughly matched buy-hold gross, lagged net of taxes/spread.** Bulk of historical outperformance came from 2000–2002 and 2008 alone. **Verdict:** Real but materially weakened in 2010s+; useful only as a risk-management overlay if you cannot stomach equity drawdowns. Net Sharpe 0.6–0.8 since 2000, lower post-2010.

### E3. Defensive low-volatility — DEAD as alpha

**Profile:** SPLV trailing 10-yr Sharpe 0.57 vs SPY 0.97; USMV 10-yr CAGR 10.4% vs SPY 15.1%. Novy-Marx & Velikov (2022, *JFE*, "Betting Against Betting Against Beta") show BAB has major implementation issues — properly-implemented Sharpe is much lower than headline 0.78. Cederburg & O'Doherty (2016, *JF*) — conditional alpha of BAB not statistically distinguishable from zero. Bali, Brown, Murray & Tang (2025, *JFE*) find BAB Sharpe rises after low-vol months — opposite of the leverage-constraints prediction; institutional demand explains returns. **Verdict: dead as alpha.** Real as defensive sleeve only — pay for lower drawdowns with lower CAGR.

### E4. Quality factor (QUAL) — DEAD as standalone

**Profile:** QUAL ETF 10-year CAGR 12.65% vs SPY 17.88%; trailing Sharpe 0.69 vs SPY 0.81; **underperformed SPY on every metric since 2013 launch.** 2022: QUAL -20.5% vs SPY -19% — failed as defense. QUAL holdings 80%+ overlap with SPY top names; correlation ~0.95+. **Verdict:** Long-only quality is essentially long beta. Skip.

### E5. Vol-targeting overlay (drawdown shape, not alpha)

Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison & Van Hemert (2018, *JPM*, "Impact of Volatility Targeting"): SPX 10% vol-targeted Sharpe 0.50 vs unscaled 0.42 (1926–2017) — Sharpe lift ~0.08. Excess kurtosis cut from 4.6 to 1.8. Liu, Tang & Zhou (2019) show Moreira-Muir (2017) and Harvey et al. (2018) results contain look-ahead via ex-post scaling factors. **Verdict:** Real but tiny as alpha; useful as risk control. Drawdown profile improvement is real (left-tail thinning); Sharpe lift alone does not clear 0.8. Best as a position-sizing layer on top of trend/momentum, not standalone.

### E6. 13F replication / cloning (mediocre)

Verbeek & Wang (2013, *JBF*) — copycat funds perform comparably to targets net of costs; cross-sectional dispersion large. GURU, ALFA, GVIP mixed live records. **45-day reporting lag is the killer:** Q4 positions are 6–10 weeks stale by Q1 disclosure. Long-only; misses short book and derivatives. **Verdict:** Mediocre. Marginal positive expected alpha at best (~1–2%); more diversification than edge. Better as stock-idea generation than systematic strategy.

## Category F — Confirmed dead or infeasible for retail (autopsies)

**Index reconstitution front-running — DEAD.** Greenwood & Sammon (2022/2024, NBER w30748) — S&P 500 inclusion abnormal return fell from **7.4% in the 1990s to 0.3–1% over 2010–2020**; deletions from large-negative to **0.1%**, despite indexation tripling. Same pattern for other index families. Wall Street desks built dedicated index-trading teams. Russell rebalance has a faint pulse but predictable cadence is fully arbitraged. **Skip.**

**SPAC arbitrage — DEAD for net new capital.** Pre-DA SPAC arb yields are now indistinguishable from T-bills (~5–6%) with bad-deal tail risk. Post-DA SPAC equity is catastrophic — Ritter (UF) data 2012–2024: de-SPAC returns below market every year, by up to 73.6%; >90% trade below $10. 2025 resurgence (138 SPACs, $25.8B per FTI) operates under stricter SEC rules (July 2024 reforms). Pre-DA arb edge is slimmer than 2020–21. **Verdict: avoid entirely.**

**Short-squeeze long-side strategies — NEGATIVE EV.** Boehmer, Jones & Zhang (2008, *JF* and 2021 global) — high short interest predicts **negative** returns ~3.8 bps/day over 20 days; shorts are informed. The 2021 GME/AMC squeeze was a one-off coordination event. Days-to-cover filters generate mostly false positives. Algorithmic short books now hedge gamma aggressively. **Verdict: retail trap.** Asymmetric option payoff is the only viable expression, expected Sharpe <0.3.

**Treasury cash-futures basis — INFEASIBLE for retail.** Fed/OFR (2023–2024 FEDS Notes; Barth-Kahn) — hedge fund positions $317B–$1T at 20–50x leverage. Retail cannot finance cash Treasuries in repo; IBKR margin financing (5–7%) destroys the trade vs 5–15 bps annualized basis. March 2020 unwind required Fed intervention. **Strict no.**

Capital-controls bind retail. **Verdict: use kimchi premium as sentiment indicator only; do not pre-position capital on suspect venues.**

## Closing argument

The honest map of retail algo alpha in 2026 is short and unglamorous. The McLean-Pontiff decay rate (58%) and Chen-Velikov post-cost decay (93%) are the right priors — most of the published factor zoo has been competed away. What remains is structurally protected: edges where institutional capital is mandate-blocked (microcap, small-CEF, niche futures), where retail-specific behavioral counterparties keep providing (Form 4 cluster signals, retail-driven earnings IV mispricings), where the risk premium pays for genuine left-tail exposure most retail and institutional players won't bear (put-write, VRP, managed-futures trend), and where the deal-by-deal nature defeats scale (merger arb, spinoffs). The most useful mental model is **"who must keep providing this trade, and why can't they stop?"** — if you cannot name a structurally-bound counterparty, you almost certainly do not have alpha. You have a backtest. The López de Prado toolkit (DSR, PBO, CPCV) and disciplined paper→small-live→full-live progression are how you find out which is which before the market tells you.
